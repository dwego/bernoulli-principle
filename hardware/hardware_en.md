# Hardware

This directory contains the mechanical files, CAD models, and assembly instructions for a school physics project created to demonstrate how a wing generates lift.

The mechanical assembly consists of three main parts:

- an experimental wing;
- two cup anemometers;
- a wind tunnel made from a PVC pipe.

The `cad/` directory should contain `.STEP`

The `stl/` directory should contain the models prepared for 3D printing.

## Experimental wing

<p align="center">
  <img src="./images/wing.png" alt="Experimental wing CAD model" width="80%">
</p>

The wing was designed with an approximately flat lower surface and a curved upper surface, creating an aerodynamic profile.

During the experiment, air flows around the wing inside the wind tunnel. One anemometer is positioned above the wing and another below it, allowing the average airflow speed in both regions to be compared.

### Features

- approximate width: `20 cm`;
- projected area used in the calculations: `120 cm²`;
- manufactured by 3D printing;
- curved upper profile;
- lower surface used as the height reference.

### Orientation

The rounded leading edge must face the incoming airflow. The thinner trailing edge must point toward the wind tunnel outlet.

The wing should be centered inside the tunnel and firmly mounted so it does not move during the test.

## Anemometer

<p align="center">
  <img src="./images/anemometer.png" alt="Cup anemometer CAD model" width="75%">
</p>

The anemometer uses three arms distributed around a central axis. Each arm contains a hollow hemisphere that captures the airflow and produces rotation.

The central axis is connected to a through-bore incremental encoder, which measures pulses and allows the rotational speed to be calculated in RPM.

### Features

- three cups;
- approximately `120°` angular spacing;
- approximate assembly radius: `5 cm`;
- central shaft connected to the encoder;
- components manufactured by 3D printing.

### Mechanical assembly

1. Print the central hub, three arms, and three cups.
2. Attach one cup to the end of each arm.
3. Orient all cups in the same rotational direction.
4. Attach the arms to the central hub with uniform spacing.
5. Install the assembly on the encoder shaft.
6. Confirm that the rotor spins freely without touching the support.
7. Check that the assembly is balanced before collecting measurements.

The anemometer should rotate with as little friction as possible. Friction, misalignment, or differences in cup mass can affect the measurements.

## PVC wind tunnel

<p align="center">
  <img src="./images/pvc-wind-tunnel.png" alt="PVC wind tunnel CAD model" width="75%">
</p>

The wind tunnel uses a PVC pipe to guide the airflow generated by a fan around the wing.

### Features

- approximate diameter: `24 cm`;
- cylindrical shape;
- internal space for the wing and both anemometers;
- fan installed at one end;
- open airflow outlet at the opposite end.

The pipe helps guide the airflow and reduce external interference. However, it does not create perfectly uniform airflow and may generate turbulence near the walls.

## Component positioning

A recommended arrangement inside the tunnel is:

```text
Air inlet
    ↓
[ Fan ]
    ↓
[ Top anemometer ]
[       Wing        ]
[ Bottom anemometer ]
    ↓
Air outlet
```

The two anemometers should be positioned at equivalent points along the wing chord:

- one above the upper surface;
- one below the lower surface.

They must not touch the wing or be placed excessively close to the pipe walls.

## Complete assembly

### 1. Preparing the parts

Before assembly:

- print the wing and anemometer parts;
- remove supports and burrs;
- test all fittings;
- confirm that both rotors spin freely;
- check that the wing fits inside the PVC pipe.

### 2. Installing the wing

1. Mark the center of the pipe.
2. Position the wing with the rounded leading edge facing the fan.
3. Keep the wing approximately level.
4. Secure the wing using side mounts or a lower support.
5. Avoid using a support that blocks a significant part of the airflow.

### 3. Installing the anemometers

1. Position one anemometer above the wing.
2. Position the second anemometer below the wing.
3. Keep both at equivalent locations along the chord.
4. Leave enough clearance for the rotors to spin.
5. Secure the encoders to prevent vibration and movement.

### 4. Installing the fan

1. Attach the fan to the inlet of the pipe.
2. Confirm that the airflow is directed toward the wing.
3. Avoid large gaps between the fan and the pipe.
4. Keep cables and supports outside the main airflow region.

### 5. Installing the electronics

Connect the encoders to the ESP32 according to the schematic available in:

- [`../electronics/`](../electronics/)

The firmware used to read the encoders is available in:

- [`../firmware/`](../firmware/)

## 3D-printing recommendations

Suggested starting settings:

- material: PETG or ABS;
- layer height: `0.20 mm`;
- infill: `15%` to `25%`;
- wall count: at least `3`;
- supports: according to part orientation;
- additional fit clearance: approximately `0.2 mm`.

These values are only starting references and may need to be adjusted for the printer and material being used.

Use enough walls on the anemometer arms to reduce deformation. Avoid excessive infill in the wing if the total mass is important to the experiment.

## Mechanical limitations

The assembly has several experimental limitations:

- the fan may not produce uniform airflow;
- the pipe walls may affect the flow;
- the anemometers measure velocity at fixed points;
- shaft friction affects rotation;
- differences between printed parts may affect calibration;
- vibration may produce unstable readings;
- wing and sensor supports also interfere with the airflow.

For this reason, the results represent an experimental approximation of the average velocity above and below the wing.
